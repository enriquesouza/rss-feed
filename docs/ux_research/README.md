# UX Research — Evidence Base for the Portal

This directory is the evidence base for the portal's UI/UX: every layout, color, and typography decision in the app must trace back to a cited finding in one of these documents, and nothing gets applied without a source. Where the evidence is mixed, heuristic, or practitioner-consensus-only, the docs say so explicitly (each has an evidence-strength section) — the goal is not to collect opinions but to separate what is measured (eye-tracking corpora, contrast math, controlled reading studies) from what is merely popular. The research target is this specific app: a single-reader, PT-BR, Axum+HTMX news portal with a 3-column layout (nav rail 240px | center feed | context sidebar 320px), a newspaper-style capa (lead headline + "destaques" list, then per-subject sections), dark theme default on `#0b0f14` with teal `#58e7d6` / amber `#e8a33d` accents, and a full light theme.

## What we change in OUR portal

Prioritized by expected impact on the reported problem (poor reading comfort), then by scan-behavior wins, then polish. Effort: S = CSS/template tweak, M = template restructuring, L = multi-file redesign.

1. **Raise body text: 15px → 16px for interface text, 18px for the reading/summary view.** Current 15px sits below every modern reference (16px browser/Material Body Large, 17pt Apple Body) and is a plausible direct cause of the discomfort. Evidence: [type_hierarchy.md §4](type_hierarchy.md#4-body-size--the-evidence) ([Smashing Magazine](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2011/10/16-pixels-body-copy-anything-less-costly-mistake/), [Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)). Effort: **S**
2. **Lift dark-theme meta/secondary text to ≥ `#aeb8c6`.** Mid-grays like `#8a94a3` on `#0b0f14` pass WCAG (6.3:1) but fail APCA for small text (Lc −44 vs the ≥60 needed) — the classic dark-mode false pass, and the other prime comfort suspect. Evidence: [colors.md §6.1/§7](colors.md#61-dark-theme--current-colors) ([APCA in a Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html)). Effort: **S**
3. **Kill pure white text on dark: headlines → `#e8e8e8`, body/summaries → `#dde3ea`.** `#fff` on `#0b0f14` measures Lc −107, past APCA's ~Lc −90 dark-mode ceiling — halation risk; Material caps high-emphasis dark-theme text at white @ 87% for the same reason. Evidence: [colors.md §6.1/§7](colors.md#7-rules-for-our-app-keep--fix) ([APCA Easy Intro](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCAeasyIntro.html), [Material dark-theme codelab](https://codelabs.developers.google.com/codelabs/design-material-darktheme)). Effort: **S**
4. **Theme-split the accents.** Light theme: teal → `#0f766e`, amber → `#92600a`/`#b45309` for any text-size use — the current accents on light paper measure 1.4:1 and 2.1:1, hard failures. Dark theme: small amber text → `#f0b25a` (plain `#e8a33d` is borderline under Lc 60 at small sizes). Per-section accents get the same two-lightness treatment, capped at ≤6 hues. Evidence: [colors.md §6.2/§7](colors.md#62-light-theme--the-real-problem) ([W3C 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [Apple HIG Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/foundations/color/)). Effort: **S**
5. **Enforce measure and leading in the center column: text blocks `max-width: 62ch`, body/dek line-height 1.5–1.6, heds 1.1–1.3.** 45–80 characters per line is the converged evidence band (Baymard 50–75, Bringhurst ~66, WCAG AAA ≤80); the sidebar's short measure runs tighter (1.45). Evidence: [type_hierarchy.md §5](type_hierarchy.md#5-line-height--line-length-interplay) ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/line-length-readability), [W3C 1.4.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/visual-presentation.html)). Effort: **S**
6. **Engineer the layer-cake: make each per-subject section header an unmistakable, descriptive stripe** — 13px UPPERCASE `h2.section-label`, 700, +0.08em, per-section accent color + hairline rule, front-loaded wording. Distinct descriptive headings are what flip scanning from the F failure mode into the most effective pattern NN/g measured. Evidence: [reading_patterns.md §3.3](reading_patterns.md#33-layer-cake-pattern--the-one-to-engineer-for) + [type_hierarchy.md §9](type_hierarchy.md#9-the-spec--concrete-hierarchy-for-the-rss-feed-portal) ([NN/g Layer-Cake](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/)). Effort: **M**
7. **Implement the semantic outline + Major Third scale from the spec table:** one `h1` (masthead on the capa, story hed on article pages) → `h2` per section (capa hed = `h2.hed-capa` at 39px) → `h3` lead cards (25px) → `h4` dense rows (16px/600 — weight, not size, separates them from body); no skipped levels; visual rank via classes. Evidence: [type_hierarchy.md §1, §9](type_hierarchy.md#9-the-spec--concrete-hierarchy-for-the-rss-feed-portal) ([W3C WAI](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/page-structure/headings/), [WebAIM Survey #10](https://webaim.org/projects/screenreadersurvey10/)). Effort: **M**
8. **One fixed kicker→hed→dek→meta stack on every card and row, with metadata *after* the headline, never before.** The F-stem fixates only leading words, so a timestamp/source prefix would consume the entire scanning budget; the kicker is a `p`/`span`, never a heading. Evidence: [type_hierarchy.md §3](type_hierarchy.md#3-the-newspaper-editorial-stack--names-and-functions) + [reading_patterns.md §7 rule 4](reading_patterns.md#7-translation-layout-rules-for-this-portal) ([NN/g First 2 Words](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/first-2-words-a-signal-for-scanning/), [Greger on kickers](https://sebastiangreger.net/2022/01/how-to-correctly-mark-up-a-headline-kicker)). Effort: **M**
9. **Front-load every PT-BR headline: entity/topic in the first 1–2 words** ("Petrobras corta…", "Fed sinaliza…"); ban "Entenda por que…"/"Saiba como…" openers — this is a rule for the AI summary/draft pipeline, not just CSS. Scanners read ~2 words per list item; generic openers scored 15% comprehension in testing. Evidence: [reading_patterns.md §6](reading_patterns.md#6-headlines-the-first-2-words--11-characters-rule) ([NN/g First 2 Words](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/first-2-words-a-signal-for-scanning/)). Effort: **S**
10. **Capa geometry check: lead story top-left of the center column, "destaques" list to its right — never mirrored.** Top-left is the measured entry point for Latin scripts and attention splits 80/20 left/right. Evidence: [reading_patterns.md §7 rule 1](reading_patterns.md#7-translation-layout-rules-for-this-portal) ([NN/g Horizontal Attention Leans Left](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/horizontal-attention-leans-left/)). Effort: **S**
11. **Demote the 320px right sidebar to ambient-only content** (weather, tickers, "related") and strip colored-background boxed modules there — anything must-see moves to the center column; boxed/flashy right-rail modules pattern-match ads and get skipped (one NN/g right rail: 25% of area, 0.8% of fixations). Evidence: [reading_patterns.md §5](reading_patterns.md#5-sidebar-blindness-the-real-risk-in-a-3-column-layout) ([NN/g Banner Blindness](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/banner-blindness-old-and-new-findings/)). Effort: **M**
12. **Ruthless consistency: identical section anatomy across all subjects, thumbnails on one fixed side of dense rows.** Varying layouts force pinball re-assessment and raise cognitive load for a daily repeat reader; alternating images trap fixations. Evidence: [reading_patterns.md §3 (pinball/zigzag)](reading_patterns.md#3-the-scanning-patterns-from-worst-to-best) ([NN/g Pinball](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/pinball-pattern-search-behavior/), [NN/g Zigzag](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/zigzag-page-layout/)). Effort: **S**
13. **Add `text-wrap: balance` to all heds and `text-wrap: pretty` to deks/body paragraphs** — kills one-word-orphan capa headlines at zero content cost; degrades gracefully. Evidence: [type_hierarchy.md §8](type_hierarchy.md#8-text-wrap-balance-for-headlines) ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/css-text-wrap-balance)). Effort: **S**
14. **Caps whitelist: UPPERCASE only for kickers, section labels, and nav-rail items — ≤2–3 words, 11–13px, +0.06–0.08em tracking; never heds, deks, or sentence-length text.** Continuous caps read ~10–20% slower, but single-word glanceable labels are actually faster in caps. Evidence: [type_hierarchy.md §7](type_hierarchy.md#7-all-caps--the-reading-cost-and-when-caps-are-fine) ([NN/g Glanceable Fonts](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/glanceable-fonts/), [The Team W](https://www.blog.theteamw.com/2017/11/03/revisiting-all-uppercase-letters-vs-upper-and-lower-case/)). Effort: **S**
15. **Non-text 3:1 audit: section accent bars, focus rings, input borders, and card outlines on `#0b0f14` must measure ≥3:1 against the adjacent surface** — elevation tints alone (~1.1:1) do not count as component boundaries. Evidence: [colors.md §7 fix 5](colors.md#7-rules-for-our-app-keep--fix) ([W3C 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html)). Effort: **S**

## What we deliberately do NOT do

1. **We do not design for (or accept) the F-pattern.** "Users read in an F shape, so put things along the F" is a popular misreading: NN/g ranks the F as the *least effective* scanning pattern — "the default pattern when there are no strong cues," i.e., a failure mode. An F heatmap on our capa means the hierarchy failed; we engineer the layer-cake instead ([NN/g Text Scanning Patterns](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/text-scanning-patterns-eyetracking/), [NN/g F-Pattern 2017](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)).
2. **We do not design for a right-to-left "reading diagonal."** For Latin-script PT-BR the measured entry point is top-LEFT with an 80/20 left/right attention split; mirrored (top-right) scanning applies only to RTL scripts like Arabic/Hebrew ([NN/g Horizontal Attention Leans Left](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/horizontal-attention-leans-left/), [NN/g F-Pattern 2017 — mirrored F for Arabic](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/)).
3. **We do not maximize contrast in the dark theme.** "More contrast is always better" is false on dark backgrounds: APCA documents a dark-mode ceiling around Lc −90, past which bright text halates — which is why `#fff` (Lc −107 on our ink) goes away, not up ([APCA Easy Intro](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCAeasyIntro.html), [Material dark-theme codelab](https://codelabs.developers.google.com/codelabs/design-material-darktheme)).
4. **We do not treat a WCAG 2.x pass as proof the dark theme is readable.** The WCAG 2 ratio "far overstates contrast for dark colors" — our own `#8a94a3` measurement (WCAG pass, APCA fail) is the concrete case — so WCAG 2.2 AA stays the compliance floor while APCA Lc is the dark-theme design check ([APCA in a Nutshell](https://git.apcacontrast.com/documentation/APCA_in_a_Nutshell.html), [W3C 1.4.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)).
5. **We do not repeat the "50% of people have astigmatism, so dark mode is unreadable" claim, nor the "Medium's 21px body raised reading time 40%" claim, as established fact.** The astigmatism prevalence is real but lower (~40.4% of adults, meta-analysis) and the specific reading-harm citation is untraceable; the Medium number is secondhand with no published methodology. Both are directional at best, and our docs flag them as such ([Hashemi et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2452232517300227), [H. Locke on the untraceable citation](https://medium.com/@h_locke/why-dark-mode-causes-more-accessibility-issues-than-it-solves-54cddf6466f5), [DeveloperUX — the 40% claim](https://developerux.com/2025/02/12/typography-in-ux-best-practices-guide/)).
